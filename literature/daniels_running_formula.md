# Daniels' Running Formula

Foundational text. The VDOT system and the E/M/T/I/R training-pace taxonomy come from this book. The coach should cite Daniels when prescribing pace zones derived from a recent race result.

## Key concepts

- **VDOT.** A single-number summary of current running fitness derived from race performance. Used to look up training paces in tables 5.1–5.2 of the book. Stronger and more practically useful than VO2max measured in a lab.
- **The five training paces.**
  - **E (easy):** conversational, ~59–74% VO2max, RPE 4–5/10
  - **M (marathon):** marathon race pace, ~75–84% VO2max
  - **T (threshold):** "comfortably hard," 30–60 minutes max sustainable, ~85–88% VO2max
  - **I (intervals):** 5K race pace, 3–8 minute reps with equal recovery, ~95–100% VO2max
  - **R (repetitions):** ~mile race pace, short reps (200m–600m) with full recovery, anaerobic
- **Training plans by goal.** Daniels publishes plans for 800m through marathon. Volume and intensity are tied to current VDOT, not to fixed paces.
- **The 24-hour rule.** A workout's training stimulus is scaled by intensity AND duration. A 30-minute T-pace workout and a 90-minute E-pace workout produce comparable training effects in different systems.

## How the coach uses this

- Pace prescription: derive VDOT from a recent race time, then prescribe E/M/T/I/R paces from the corresponding table.
- Workout structure: when prescribing intervals, use Daniels' standard structures (e.g., 6×800m at I-pace with equal-time jog recoveries; 3×1.5 mi at T-pace with 60s rest).
- Long run: 25–30% of weekly volume, capped at 150 min for most runners (longer for marathon prep).

## Limitations / caveats

- VDOT assumes a recent maximal effort. If the athlete hasn't raced or done an all-out time trial, VDOT-derived paces are estimates.
- VDOT tables don't account for terrain, heat, altitude — adjust by feel/HR.
- Daniels' approach is intensity-first; for ultra distances, Pfitzinger's volume-first approach may be a better starting point.
