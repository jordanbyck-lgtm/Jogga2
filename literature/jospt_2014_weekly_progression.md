# JOSPT 2014 — Weekly progression and injury subtype

A cohort study of recreational runners examining the relationship between week-over-week training progression and the development of specific running-related injuries.

> **NOTE on this seed file.** Citation is approximate (2014 JOSPT cohort on progression and injury subtype). The coach should verify the precise citation via web search at first invocation and update this file with the exact title, sample size, and effect sizes. The general finding — that >30% week-over-week increases are associated with elevated risk for specific injury subtypes — is well established and reproduced in multiple analyses.

## Key findings

- Week-over-week mileage increases >30% are associated with elevated incidence of:
  - Patellofemoral pain syndrome
  - Iliotibial band syndrome
  - Medial tibial stress syndrome ("shin splints")
- Below the 30% threshold, the dose–response relationship between progression and injury is weaker than the popular "10% rule" implies.
- Different injury types respond to different volume-related stressors (single-run distance vs. cumulative weekly volume).

## What this is NOT

- This is **not** the source of the "10% rule." That rule is folk wisdom from popular running media (often attributed to Joe Henderson at Runner's World) and has thin empirical backing.
- The 30% threshold is a meaningful inflection point in the data, NOT a hard physiological limit. Athletes routinely tolerate 30%+ increases when other context (recovery, prior fitness, single-run progression) is favorable.

## How the coach uses this

- Anchors the `progression_weekly` strong guideline.
- Used to push back on the "10% rule" myth and substitute a better-supported threshold.

## Caveats

- Cohort design — observational, can show association but not causation.
- The dominant signal in more recent literature (BJSM 2025) is single-run progression, not weekly. Weekly progression is the secondary signal.
