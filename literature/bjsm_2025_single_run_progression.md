## Claim

Single-run distance jumps — specifically, runs that exceed the longest run of the prior 30 days by more than ~10% — are the dominant risk factor for running-related injury in recreational runners. This effect is stronger than week-over-week mileage change, and stronger than acute:chronic workload ratio (ACWR) measured at the weekly level.

## Implications

- The most common practical mistake — long-run jumps — is also the most consequential.
- Weekly mileage targets matter less than the structure of how that mileage is distributed across days, especially the longest single session.
- Coaches should track and compare upcoming long runs against the runner's prior 30-day max.

## How the coach uses this

- Explicitly **not** the "10% rule." This is the 10% rule applied to single-run distance only, with empirical support, NOT to weekly mileage.
