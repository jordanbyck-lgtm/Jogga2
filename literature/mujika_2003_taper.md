# Mujika & Padilla 2003 — Scientific bases for precompetition tapering

The standard meta-analytic reference for taper structure. Synthesizes physiological and performance data across endurance sports to identify the taper variables that matter.

## Key findings

- **Volume reduction is the primary lever.** Volume reductions of 41–60% over the taper window produce the largest performance gains. Smaller reductions (<30%) are often insufficient; larger reductions (>60%) start to produce detraining.
- **Intensity should be MAINTAINED, not reduced.** Cutting intensity during taper degrades the performance benefit. Keep some quality work; reduce its volume.
- **Frequency should largely be maintained.** Don't suddenly add rest days. Athletes accustomed to running 6 days/week should mostly continue running 6 days/week; the runs are shorter and easier overall.
- **Taper duration scales with the prior training load and the race distance.** Longer races → longer taper. More accumulated fatigue → longer taper.

## Performance gain estimate

Athletes can expect ~3% performance improvement from a well-structured taper, which is substantial in endurance sports where margins are narrow.

## How the coach uses this

- Anchors the `taper_duration` strong guideline.
- Justifies maintaining quality intensity during taper while cutting volume.

## Caveats

- Meta-analysis from 2003. More recent data largely confirms the framework but adds nuance for ultra-distance events (longer tapers may be appropriate).
- Inter-individual variability in taper response is substantial. Some athletes peak with shorter tapers, some with longer. Trust the athlete's prior data when they have it.
