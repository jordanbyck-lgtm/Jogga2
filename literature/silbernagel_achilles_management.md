# Silbernagel et al. — Achilles tendinopathy management

> **NOTE.** Several Silbernagel papers exist in this space; this seed file references the foundational pain-monitoring + continued activity RCT and the broader body of Silbernagel's work on Achilles tendinopathy management. The coach should confirm specific citations via web search when citing detail.

## Key findings

- Continued participation in running and sport during Achilles tendinopathy rehabilitation, **provided pain stays within an acceptable range**, does not worsen outcomes and may improve adherence and long-term recovery vs. complete rest.
- The "pain-monitoring model" (modified VISA-A or 0–10 pain scale, with thresholds for stop / modify / continue) gives the runner an actionable rule for managing daily training during recovery.
- Eccentric loading (heel drops) and heavy slow resistance (HSR) are the most evidence-supported rehab interventions.

## Operational thresholds (rough guide — not from a specific paper)

- Pain ≤ 2/10 during run AND not worse 24 hr after: continue.
- Pain 3–5/10: modify (reduce volume, drop intensity).
- Pain ≥ 6/10, OR worse the next morning, OR pain at rest, OR swelling: stop running, transition to loading-only rehab, consult PT if persistent.

## How the coach uses this

- Anchors the `acute_achilles_no_speedwork` hard stop.
- Justifies the conservative bias on speedwork during any Achilles symptoms while allowing easy running with pain monitoring during chronic mild presentations.
- Supports prescription of isometric or eccentric calf loading as part of the modified plan.

## Caveats

- Pain-monitoring approaches require the athlete to be honest with themselves and pull back when needed. For athletes who chronically push through pain, default conservative.
- Acute cases (sharp pain, swelling, recent onset) are different from chronic mild tendinopathy and the hard stop applies.
