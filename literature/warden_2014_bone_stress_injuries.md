# Warden et al. 2014 — Bone stress injury management

> **NOTE.** A foundational JOSPT review on bone stress injury (BSI) in distance runners. The coach should confirm exact citation via web search when citing specifics.

## Key concepts

- **Bone stress injury** is a continuum: from stress reaction (early) to stress fracture (advanced). Early identification and load reduction matter enormously.
- **High-risk sites** — femoral neck, anterior tibial cortex, navicular, sesamoids, fifth metatarsal — require longer return-to-run timelines and conservative imaging-confirmed clearance.
- **Lower-risk sites** — posterior medial tibia, fibula, calcaneus, second to fourth metatarsals — typically heal well with relative rest.
- **Risk factors** include rapid training progression, low energy availability (RED-S), low bone mineral density, biomechanical asymmetries, prior BSI history, female sex (especially with menstrual dysfunction).

## Return-to-run progression

Standard graded protocol after medical clearance:
1. **Walking only** (1–2 weeks). Pain-free walking is the precondition for any running.
2. **Walk-jog intervals** (2–4 weeks). Start with very short jog intervals (e.g., 1 min jog / 4 min walk), progressively reduce walk:jog ratio.
3. **Continuous easy runs** (2–4 weeks). Short (15–25 min) on alternate days, easy pace, monitoring symptoms.
4. **Re-establish baseline volume** (4–6 weeks). Gradually rebuild to pre-injury easy volume.
5. **Reintroduce quality** only after baseline volume is restored and no symptoms have occurred for ≥4 weeks.

## How the coach uses this

- Anchors the `return_to_run_post_stress_fracture` hard stop.
- Defines the structure of the graded walk-jog progression after clearance.
- Provides risk-factor list for recognizing athletes at elevated BSI risk during onboarding (low energy availability, prior BSI history, etc.) — flag in `harness/PHYSIOLOGY.md` or `CONSTRAINTS.md`.

## Caveats

- High-risk sites (femoral neck, anterior tibia, navicular) require sports medicine specialist clearance, not generalist clearance.
- For athletes with recurrent BSI, address upstream causes (energy availability, biomechanics, bone density) — not just the injury cycle.
