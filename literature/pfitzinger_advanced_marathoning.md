# Advanced Marathoning

The standard reference for marathon-specific training, especially for runners targeting sub-3:30 to sub-2:30. Pfitzinger's plans are higher-volume and longer-build than Daniels' marathon plans.

## Key concepts

- **Mesocycle structure.** Plans built as 4 mesocycles: Endurance → Lactate Threshold + Endurance → Race Preparation → Taper. Each mesocycle has a distinct training emphasis.
- **Long runs as the primary stimulus.** Pfitzinger's long runs progress to 22 miles (sometimes 24), and a portion is run at "general aerobic" rather than easy pace — building durability AND aerobic ceiling.
- **Marathon-pace long runs.** A signature workout: e.g., 16 mi total with 12 mi at marathon pace. Tests fueling, pacing discipline, and durability simultaneously.
- **Lactate threshold workouts.** Long tempos (4–7 mi at LT pace) and tempo intervals (e.g., 4×2 mi at LT). More volume of LT work than Daniels prescribes.
- **Two long runs per week** in some plans (a "medium-long" of 12–14 mi midweek + a 18–22 mi weekend long run). High-volume marathoners only.

## Plan tiers

- 12-week plan, peaking 55 mpw
- 12-week plan, peaking 70 mpw
- 18-week plan, peaking 55 mpw
- 18-week plan, peaking 85+ mpw

The longer plans suit experienced runners who can sustain higher volume; the shorter plans are for runners coming in with a base.

## Tapering

3-week taper. Pfitzinger's taper structure is:
- Week -3: ~80% of peak volume, key workouts at goal pace
- Week -2: ~60% of peak volume, lighter quality
- Week -1: ~40% of peak volume, very short final tune-ups
- Last hard workout 8–10 days out from race day

## How the coach uses this

- Default reference for marathon-specific plans.
- Marathon-pace long runs as a key late-block session.
- Lactate threshold prescription (4–7 mi continuous tempos, or 2×2 mi to 4×2 mi intervals at LT pace with 90s rest).
- Taper structure for marathon distance.

## Limitations / caveats

- Pfitzinger volumes (55–85+ mpw) are appropriate for experienced runners. Don't drop a recreational mid-volume runner into a 70mpw plan.
- The "general aerobic" pace is a useful concept but it's the most-debated piece of his methodology — easy to drift into junk-pace territory if the athlete doesn't have HR discipline.
