# Recovery week cadence

## Why
Adaptation happens during recovery, not during stress. Pushing >5 weeks of progressive load without a cutback compounds fatigue and elevates injury and overreaching risk.

## What a recovery week looks like
- Total volume: 70–80% of the prior 4-week average
- Intensity: drop one quality session, easier paces on remaining quality
- Long run: shorter (60–70% of recent peak long run)
- Strides and hill reps are fine
- Rest day count: same or +1
- Sleep emphasis: tell the athlete to use the cutback to bank sleep

## What the coach does
- Plan a recovery week every 3–4 weeks by default for build phases. Every 4–5 weeks for athletes with high training age and consistent recent history.
- If 5+ weeks have passed without one: rule fires. Insert the cutback this week, even if it interrupts a planned block.

## When override is reasonable
- Athlete is already in a taper or post-race recovery — that period serves the same function.
- Athlete is at very low volume and the natural variability already provides cutbacks.
- Block is structured as 6/1 (six on, one off) for an experienced athlete who has historically tolerated it well.
